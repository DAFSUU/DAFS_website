Showcase: four years of the European Parliament

What text mining can tell us about 40,000 plenary speeches in 24 languages, 2019–2023

Author

Janpieter van der Pol

The European Parliament publishes every speech given in its plenary. In the ninth term (July 2019 to the elections of 2024) that is more than 50,000 speeches, in 24 languages, on everything from the pandemic and the war in Ukraine to the Green Deal and the rule of law in Hungary. This page shows what the methods from this course can do with that record. The analysis covers 39,617 speeches by 842 MEPs from July 2019 to September 2023, where the data ends.

It follows the same steps as the showcase on Dutch municipal councils, at a different scale: one parliament instead of 297 councils, 24 languages instead of one, and a map of Europe instead of the Netherlands.

The analysis

The report is interactive: hover over a bar, a cell, a dot or a region for details, and use the drop-down menus to switch groups, issues, domains and countries. Open the report in its own window for more room.

How it was built

Step Method What it does Keep in mind
1. One language Machine translation (in the source data) Every speech has an English version, so 24 languages can be modelled together 78% of the English texts are machine translations; names and idioms suffer most
2. Who is speaking Entity linking to the Parliament’s open data (data.europarl.europa.eu) Links each speaker to their country and national party 99% of speeches are matched; a few names are spelled differently in the two sources
3. Topics BERTopic with Rhobots: sentence embeddings, UMAP, HDBSCAN, c-TF-IDF; labels and policy domains from a local LLM 209 topics in 18 policy domains The LLM put most war topics under foreign affairs; a simple rule moves them to their own domain
4. Positions Zero-shot stance detection with a local LLM, which first writes its reason For nine contested issues, judges whether a speech argues for or against a statement A first version without reasons and decision notes gave implausible results; read the table of national parties with care
5. Sustainability Zero-shot classification into the 17 Sustainable Development Goals, plus a dictionary of frames Shows who talks about which goals, how the energy crisis displaced climate, where in Europe, and whether climate is framed as a cost, an emergency or an opportunity The goal descriptions and the similarity threshold shape the result; goal 16 almost never reaches it
6. Places, countries, organisations Named entity recognition (WikiNEuRal) and a gazetteer (GeoNames) Finds 579 EU places, mapped to NUTS 2 regions, plus countries and organisations Country names are also village names (America in Limburg, Cuba in Portugal), and Brussels usually means the institutions

Councils and the European Parliament compared

Municipal councils European Parliament
Documents 1 million council documents, 135,000 motions and questions 40,000 speeches
Who 297 councils, national and local parties 842 MEPs, 8 political groups, 200+ national parties
Hardest step Finding out who submitted a document (signature blocks) Comparing 24 languages (machine translation)
Geography Where the council is (provinces, NUTS 2) Where the MEP comes from (member states) and which regions the speeches name (NUTS 2)
Positions From the motions parties submit From what MEPs argue in their speeches

Questions to think about

  1. Most of the English text is machine-translated. Which steps of the pipeline are most sensitive to translation errors: topics, positions, or named entities? How would you test it?
  2. The number of speeches a group gives depends on its size and on how speaking time is shared. Why does lift not suffer from this, and what does it hide?
  3. The positions come from what MEPs say. Roll-call votes record what they do. When would the two differ, and which would you trust for which question?
  4. Brussels is named in almost 1,000 speeches, but rarely as a city. How would you tell the place from the institutions it stands for?
NoteData and code

Speeches: European Parliament plenary, ninth term, with English versions (NetworkIsLifeR collection). MEPs: European Parliament Open Data API. Topic model and NER: Rhobots. Local language model: qwen3 through Ollama. Places: GeoNames (CC BY 4.0). Regions and maps: Eurostat GISCO (NUTS 2024).
